Knowledge

Print your own spare parts

A broken clip, a cover, a gear: many parts can be reprinted. Some cannot. An honest overview.

  • Free
  • No sign-up
  • No data sent: inputs stay in your browser

Where reprinting pays off

  • Covers, clips, knobs, brackets and handles
  • Household and hobby parts without a safety function
  • Parts that are no longer sold
  • Adapters, spacers, cable guides

Where you should not print

Anything with a safety function: brakes, load-bearing parts, straps and mounts that hold people. Also parts on mains power, gas, heating or pressure vessels, permanent food contact, high continuous load, heat above the material limit, or parts that must be legally approved. FDM parts are weaker along the layers and can break suddenly. When in doubt, buy the original part.

Step by step

  1. Measure. Calipers, a sketch and photos of the original part and its mounting spot.
  2. Design or download. Simple parts can be built with the generators or a CAD program. Check the licence before using third-party models.
  3. Plan the fit. Allow clearance using the tolerance guide values and print a test piece.
  4. Choose the material. See below.
  5. Orient. Do not place loads across the layers. Check position and size in the print bed preview.
  6. Check the cost. The cost calculator shows what the reprint costs.

Frequently asked questions

Do printed spare parts last as long as the original?

Not always. Printed parts are weaker along the layers and can fatigue under constant load. For light use they are often enough.

Which material for outdoors?

ASA resists UV and weather. PLA already deforms in the sun at about 55 to 60 °C.

May I reprint any part?

Better not for parts with a safety function. Also respect copyright and design rights for third-party models.
