What is PA-GF (glass fibre nylon) used for?

Stiff heat-resistant parts; glass fibre is less conductive than carbon

+ Pros

High stiffness and heat resistance, often tougher than carbon variants

– Cons

Abrasive (hardened nozzle), hygroscopic, enclosure needed

Printing tips at a glance

  • Nozzle temperature: 260–300 (per manufacturer) °C – start in the middle of the range on a new spool and fine-tune with a temperature tower.
  • Bed temperature: 80–120 °C.
  • An enclosed chamber is strongly recommended, otherwise expect warping and layer cracks.
  • Because of the abrasive filler a hardened nozzle (e.g. hardened steel) is a must – brass wears out quickly.
  • Drying: 80 °C, 8–12 h. Wet filament shows up as crackling, stringing and rough surfaces.

Frequently asked questions

What nozzle temperature does PA-GF (glass fibre nylon) need?

Guideline for PA-GF (glass fibre nylon): 260–300 (per manufacturer) °C nozzle and 80–120 °C bed temperature. The exact values are printed on the manufacturer's spool.

How heat resistant is PA-GF (glass fibre nylon)?

Heat deflection temperature (HDT) is around approx. 191 (PA6-GF25, annealed) °C. Above that, loaded parts start to deform.

Does PA-GF (glass fibre nylon) need to be dried?

Recommended: 80 °C, 8–12 h.

Do I need an enclosure for PA-GF (glass fibre nylon)?

An enclosed chamber is strongly recommended, otherwise expect warping and layer cracks. Because of the abrasive filler a hardened nozzle (e.g. hardened steel) is a must – brass wears out quickly.

Values are guidelines from manufacturer datasheets (Polymaker, Bambu Lab, Prusa). They may differ by manufacturer, colour and print settings.
