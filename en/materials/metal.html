What is Metal-filled filament used for?

Display/decor parts with a metal look, polishable

+ Pros

Heavy, cool to the touch, metal-like after wet sanding (600–1500) and polishing

– Cons

Very abrasive → hardened nozzle, brittle, lower strength and layer adhesion than base (Prusa)

Printing tips at a glance

  • Nozzle temperature: 190–270 (depending on base) °C – start in the middle of the range on a new spool and fine-tune with a temperature tower.
  • Bed temperature: 60–100 °C.
  • An enclosure is not needed, but avoid drafts.
  • Because of the abrasive filler a hardened nozzle (e.g. hardened steel) is a must – brass wears out quickly.

Frequently asked questions

What nozzle temperature does Metal-filled filament need?

Guideline for Metal-filled filament: 190–270 (depending on base) °C nozzle and 60–100 °C bed temperature. The exact values are printed on the manufacturer's spool.

Do I need an enclosure for Metal-filled filament?

An enclosure is not needed, but avoid drafts. Because of the abrasive filler a hardened nozzle (e.g. hardened steel) is a must – brass wears out quickly.

Values are guidelines from manufacturer datasheets (Polymaker, Bambu Lab, Prusa). They may differ by manufacturer, colour and print settings.
